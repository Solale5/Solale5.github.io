Available on the Chrome Web Store for Chrome 121+. No account required. The sample dashboard uses fictional tabs and cannot access your browser tabs.

Hours, not just days

Sort oldest-used first. Filter from 30 minutes to 90 days, with precise last-used times and quick counts.

A reason to review

Spot duplicate URLs and tabs unused for several hours. Each suggestion explains its rule. You decide what goes.

Save it or let it go

Save & close keeps a searchable URL copy. Just close is for disposable tabs. Nothing closes automatically.

Get started in Chrome

  1. Open Tab Shelf on the Chrome Web Store and click Add to Chrome.
  2. Pin Tab Shelf in Chrome’s extensions menu, then click its icon.
  3. Review unused tabs, select the links you want to keep, and choose Save & close. Find them again under Your shelf.

Already using the manual preview? Export a backup from that installation, then import it into the store version before removing the preview.

Made for everyday tab overload

I built Tab Shelf while trying to make my existing laptop more comfortable to use. The goal was simple: stop keeping every useful link loaded just because I might want it again.

The extension uses Chrome’s tab metadata and local storage, without page injections, accounts, analytics, or a server. Active, pinned, audio-playing, loading and Chrome-protected tabs are excluded from closing.

Saved links preserve URLs, not unsaved text or page state. Last-used dates are clues rather than a measure of reading time. Closing sleeping tabs may reduce clutter without freeing much additional memory.
